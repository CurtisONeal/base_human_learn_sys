## People — who is who in this passage

Add a section under the heading `## People`, placed immediately after
`## Key Concepts & Vocabulary`. It tells a reader who takes part in this
passage or is discussed in it, what the source says about them, and why they
matter to the topic — so every claim can be tied to the right person. It is a
markdown table with exactly these columns, in this order:

| person | role | affiliation & background (as stated) | relevance | citations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| <name> | <role in this source> | <what the source says about them> | <why they matter here> | [H:MM:SS] [H:MM:SS] |

Who gets a row — one row per person:

- Everyone who speaks: host, co-host, interviewer, guest, interviewee,
  speaker, panelist, moderator.
- People who are discussed substantively, quoted, or cited: an executive
  whose announcement is analysed, an author whose paper is explained, a
  researcher whose claim is debated.
- NOT companies, products, models, teams, shows or publications — those are
  terms and belong in Key Concepts & Vocabulary, even when they are named
  after a person.
- NOT private individuals (see "People — who may be named" above): giveaway
  or raffle winners, attendees, audience members, callers, people named only
  in passing. Leave them out of the table entirely.
- A public person who is only named, with nothing said about them, gets a
  row only when the passage treats the name as significant; write
  `mentioned only` in the background column.

Column rules:

- **person** — the name as the source gives it. When the transcript is
  machine-made, use its most frequent spelling and add `(ASR spelling)` after
  the name, unless the name appears in the source title, description or tags.
  Never "correct" a name to a spelling the audio cannot distinguish. A host or
  speaker the recording never names, but the SOURCE DESCRIPTION does (e.g.
  "NLW traces …"), gets that name followed by `(per source description)`. A
  speaker named nowhere is described by role, e.g. `Unnamed host`.
- **role** — the person's role IN THIS SOURCE, not their job title: one of
  host, co-host, interviewer, guest, interviewee, speaker, panelist,
  moderator, person discussed, quoted, cited author (two joined with ` / `
  if both apply). The one asking the questions is the interviewer; the one
  answering is the interviewee or guest — do not swap them.
- **affiliation & background (as stated)** — only what this source says or
  shows: title, organisation, background, credentials, prior work
  ("co-founder of X; previously led Y"). Never fill this from your own
  knowledge. If a fact the source does not give is essential to understand
  the person, add at most one short clause labelled
  `(per model knowledge, cutoff uncertain)`; otherwise leave it out. If the
  source states nothing about them, write `not stated`.
- **relevance** — one or two sentences: what this person contributes to the
  topic here — what they argue, announce, build, predict or are criticised
  for, and their stance. Attribute precisely: what the host reports someone
  else said is "reported by the host"; a quote belongs to the person who
  says it in the source; the interviewer's framing is not the guest's claim.
- **citations** — the timestamp of the line where the person is introduced
  or first named, then up to three where their key points are made. The
  citation rules above apply: timestamps from this passage only, nothing
  inside the brackets but the timestamp.

Order the rows: people who speak first, in order of first appearance; then
people discussed, in order of first mention.

If no person speaks identifiably or is discussed, still write the heading,
the header row and the separator row of the table, then a line `- None`.

The other sections keep attributing claims to people by name ("the guest
argues …"); do not copy the People table into them.
